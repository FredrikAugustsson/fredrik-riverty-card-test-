# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A 29-year-old app-first fashion and home shopper in Berlin who regularly orders several sizes and keeps one.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** To try things at home without her money leaving her account until she has decided to keep them.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** "I order three sizes, keep one. I don't want 150 € gone from my account for two weeks while I wait for a refund." (P2) The moment of misery is the checkout where Riverty isn't offered. There she must either pay upfront or reach for Klarna or PayPal, and each time that choice strengthens another brand's hold. The interviews don't state that last link explicitly, so it is inference.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Klarna, PayPal, Visa
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** She over-orders on purpose. She buys three sizes and plans to keep one. (Evidenced, P2)
She reaches checkout and looks at what is offered. She does not search for a preferred provider. (Evidenced: 68% choose from what checkout shows; P1: "I look at what's offered and pick the one that's quickest.")
If Riverty is absent, she falls back on a payment method she already holds, such as Klarna, PayPal or a Visa. (Evidenced as holdings, P3; the choice at this moment is Inferred.)
She selects the option that keeps the cash in her account longest. For her this is a deferred-pay option, not a card charged immediately. (Inferred from P2's stated motive, and 46% of the survey cite "money leaves only after I decide to keep it.")
She waits for the parcel, tries the items, and returns two. (Evidenced, P2)
She tracks the return herself. She checks that the refund or invoice adjustment lands, and settles whatever is owed. (Inferred. P10 says "I don't want to be chasing invoices," which suggests this is a known irritant.)
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Exposure when it fails. Where only upfront payment is offered, she carries the 150 € outlay for two weeks, which is the exact pain she describes.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** She waits for the parcel, tries the items, and returns two. (Evidenced, P2)
- **Your journey map, a shareable link, or the map file you committed (e.g. journey-map.html):** Here is the one-page timeline. The four stages follow, each in the format you asked for.## Future state journey: "Keep it first, pay later"

**1. Discover**
- **User Action:** Sees card offer after a checkout → learns one clear reason to want it.
- **Internal State:** Skeptical ("a fourth thing?") → needs a single benefit, not features.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Redundancy objection → "Keep it first, pay later" gives the one reason.

**2. Activate**
- **User Action:** Applies and switches on auto-repay in-app → no invoices to chase.
- **Internal State:** Wary of debt → reassured by pay-later framing, not "credit card."
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Credit anxiety and invoice chasing → fixed limit, auto-repay by default.

**3. Use anywhere**
- **User Action:** Orders three sizes at a non-partner shop → no Klarna or PayPal detour.
- **Internal State:** Confident → no checkout lottery over which method is offered.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** Checkout where Riverty is absent → the card goes where Riverty can't.

**4. Keep and settle**
- **User Action:** Keeps one item, returns two → pays only for what she kept.
- **Internal State:** In control → credits Riverty for the float, not a competitor.
- **Pain Point Addressed:** 150 € outlay for two weeks → money leaves after her decision.

## 3 competitive advantages over the manual workaround

1. **One instrument → replaces juggling Klarna, PayPal and Visa per checkout.**
2. **Guaranteed float → coverage no longer depends on what each checkout offers.**
3. **Automatic settlement → no return tracking, so the brand association stays with Riverty.**
